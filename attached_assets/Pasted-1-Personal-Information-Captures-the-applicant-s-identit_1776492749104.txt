1. Personal Information

Captures the applicant’s identity and basic details.

Typical Fields:

First Name
Last Name
Other Names
Gender
Date of Birth
Nationality
National ID / Passport Number
Marital Status (optional, depending on policy)
2. Contact Information

Ensures the applicant can be reached.

Typical Fields:

Email Address
Phone Number(s)
Alternative Contact Number
Physical Address
City / Province / District
Postal Address
3. Position Applied For

Links the application to a specific vacancy.

Typical Fields:

Job Title (auto-filled if applied from job listing)
Job Reference Number
Preferred Location (if multiple postings exist)
Availability (immediate, notice period)
4. Professional Summary / Cover Letter

Allows the applicant to introduce themselves.

Typical Fields:

Personal Statement / Summary
Cover Letter (text field or file upload)
5. Educational Background

Captures academic qualifications.

Typical Fields:

Highest Level of Education
Institution Name(s)
Qualification(s) অর্জained
Field of Study
Start and End Dates
Certifications / Professional Training
6. Work Experience

Captures employment history.

Typical Fields:

Employer Name
Job Title
Responsibilities
Start and End Dates
Reason for Leaving
Key Achievements
7. Skills and Competencies

Highlights capabilities relevant to the job.

Typical Fields:

Technical Skills
Soft Skills
Languages (with proficiency level)
Computer Literacy
Certifications / Licenses
8. Document Uploads

Supports verification and detailed review.

Typical Fields:

Curriculum Vitae (CV)
Cover Letter (if not typed)
Academic Certificates
Professional Certificates
Identification Document
Reference Letters
9. Screening Questions (Job-Specific)

Used for shortlisting and filtering candidates.

Typical Fields:

Yes/No questions (e.g., “Do you have X years of experience?”)
Multiple choice questions
Short-answer responses
Eligibility questions (e.g., work permit status)
10. References

Provides third-party validation.

Typical Fields:

Referee Name
Relationship to Applicant
Organisation
Contact Details (email, phone)
11. Compensation & Availability

Aligns expectations early.

Typical Fields:

Expected Salary
Current Salary (optional)
Notice Period
Willingness to Relocate
Preferred Work Type (full-time, contract, etc.)
12. Declarations & Compliance

Ensures legal and policy compliance.

Typical Fields:

Declaration of truthfulness
Consent to background checks
Conflict of interest disclosure
Criminal record declaration (if applicable)
Data privacy consent (aligned with data protection laws)
13. Diversity & Inclusion (Optional)

Used for reporting and equity monitoring (must be compliant with local regulations).

Typical Fields:

Disability Status
Gender Identity
Ethnicity (where appropriate and lawful)
14. System-Generated Fields (Backend)

Not visible to applicants but critical for system design.

Typical Fields:

Application ID
Submission Timestamp
Application Status
AI Score / Ranking
Recruiter Notes
Forward-Looking Enhancements (AI-Driven Forms)

A modern system should go beyond static forms by incorporating:

Dynamic Forms: Fields adapt based on the job role
Auto-fill via CV Parsing: Reduces manual entry
Smart Validation: Detects incomplete or inconsistent entries
Skill Inference: AI extracts competencies from uploaded CVs
Guided Application Assistant: Chatbot to assist applicants
Design Best Practices (Critical for Implementation)
Progressive Disclosure: Break the form into steps (multi-page wizard)
Save & Resume Later: Essential for long applications
Mobile Responsiveness: Many applicants use mobile devices
Validation at Each Step: Prevent incomplete submissions
Minimal Mandatory Fields: Reduce drop-off rates
